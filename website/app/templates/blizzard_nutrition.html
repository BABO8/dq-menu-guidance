Nutritional Takeaways & Smarter Choices (2026)

If you are watching your sugar, calorie, or carbohydrate intake while enjoying a fast-food frozen dessert:

  • Choose the Mini (6 oz): You can enjoy authentic OREO®, Butterfinger®, or Reese's® flavor for ~350 to 420 calories, saving between 700 and 900+ calories compared to ordering a Large (21 oz).
  • Fruit vs. Candy Profiles: Fruit-based Blizzards like Strawberry Cheesecake average 10% to 15% fewer calories and lower saturated fat than heavy peanut butter or fudge dough blends.
  • Request Whipped Topping Removal: Skipping the standard whipped cream garnish on Royal Blizzards or specialty parfaits saves between 50 and 80 calories and 6g of saturated fat.
  • Non-Dairy Alternative: For strict dairy avoidance, Dairy Queen offers factory-sealed Non-Dairy Dilly® Bars (220 kcal, certified vegan and gluten-free, made with coconut cream).

Blizzard Nutrition FAQs

What is the lowest calorie DQ Blizzard? +

The lowest calorie Dairy Queen Blizzard is the Mini Strawberry Cheesecake Blizzard or Mini Butterfinger® Blizzard, each containing approximately 350 calories (kcal). In general, fruit-infused treats contain less saturated fat and fewer calories than candy-loaded or double-dough creations.

How many calories are in a Mini vs. Large Blizzard? +

A Mini (6 oz) Blizzard averages between 350 and 450 calories, while a Large (21 oz) Blizzard ranges from 1,020 to 1,390 calories. Ordering a Mini instead of a Large saves an average of 650 to 950 calories and over 90 grams of added sugar per serving.

Can you order a dairy-free Blizzard at Dairy Queen? +

No, American Dairy Queen does not currently prepare Blizzards with non-dairy soft-serve machines. All soft-serve dispensed from store spouts contains cows milk. For dairy-free dining, guests can purchase pre-packaged, factory-sealed Non-Dairy Dilly® Bars made from coconut cream and vegan chocolate coating.

Where does Dairy Queen obtain its official nutritional data? +

Nutritional calculations are conducted by independent certified food testing laboratories using standard recipe formulas approved by American Dairy Queen Corporation. Minor variations occur due to handcrafted preparation and regional milk fat percentages.
